# Yogardener — research dossier & audit

Compiled 6 November 2026 for the Keystone Cognition Labs demo. Every claim used
on the demo site is listed here with its source. Anything not in this file was
treated as unverified and left off the page.

## 1. The headline correction

**Yogardener already has a website.** The original lead scan flagged Yogardener
as a "no website" prospect. That is wrong.

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Live site | `https://yogardener.com/` — WordPress + Elementor, 12 service pages, portfolio, 21-post blog, contact, 3 service-area pages |
| Second site | `https://go.yogardener.com/` — separate brochure site, same phone and address |
| Google Business Profile | `5.0 ★`, 62–66 reviews depending on crawl date; 181 photos; **two** locations sharing one phone number |
| Nextdoor business page | Nextdoor "Neighborhood Fave" **2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026** — favourite in **584 neighbourhoods** |
| Instagram / Facebook / LinkedIn | `@yogardeneratl` (965 followers) · Facebook page `Yogardener-1368018776600133` · LinkedIn `company/yogardener` (founded 2015, 1 employee) |

**Consequence for the pitch.** "You have no website" is not available as an
opening. The demo was rebuilt as a **redesign concept** — "here is what your
homepage could be" — which is an honest, stronger and more valuable offer than a
website the owner already owns. The AI-answering offer is unaffected and remains
the sharper second half of the pitch.

**Consequence for the pipeline.** The lead scanner's "no website" test is
unreliable. Yogardener was scored as a miss. That heuristic needs to be
re-verified against the actual domain before any business is contacted.

## 4. What is wrong with their current site (the pitch)

Ordered by how much it costs them.

1. **Two Google Business Profiles, one phone number.** The listing at
   1226 Zonolite Rd NE carries the 60+ reviews; a second listing at the Decatur
   Suite 400 address shows "No reviews". Split local signals, and the Decatur
   address is a virtual office on their own site.
2. **`/reviews/` renders nothing.** The homepage's "Real Testimonials" block
   links to a page with no testimonials on it. Sixty-plus five-star reviews are
   invisible on their own site.
3. **The homepage FAQ is broken.** Five questions appear; only one has an answer.
   The answers exist — on the *services* page. The homepage FAQ is a dead end.
4. **Two broken counters.** "Years of Designing Distinctive Atlanta Landscapes"
   and "Custom Landscape Installations Completed" both render **"0 +"** — the
   animation never fires. On a business founded in 2015, a zero reads as
   "zero years" and "zero projects".
5. **A competitor's testimonial is published on their site.** The "Susan Taba"
   testimonial on go.yogardener.com describes **Georgia Landscaping and
   Hardscaping LLC** and its owner "Elmer" — a different Marietta company,
   phone (404) 791-5609. The two businesses appear to share an asset source.
6. **go.yogardener.com's privacy policy still contains a third party's email**,
   `scott@peachtreeroofing.com`, a roofing company.
7. **Keyword-stuffed AI copy.** "Trusted landscaper in Atlanta", "landscape
   contractor in Atlanta" and "experienced landscape contractors in Atlanta"
   appear repeatedly in one paragraph. Development artefacts
   (`#test-popup-link5f7a313`, `elementor-action` URLs) are left in the HTML.
8. **Broken footer links** — Privacy Policy and Terms & Services both point at `#`.
9. **"Leave A Review" is an empty anchor** (`<a href="">`).
10. **No visible social proof in the body** — the 5.0 badge sits on the hero
    image; the Nextdoor award is a small image with no explanation.
11. **The community work is invisible.** Concrete Jungle, cOMingle, Atlanta
    Action Day, the Summer Jobs Program — the most distinctive thing the
    business does is on a single unfound page.
12. **The portfolio has no detail.** Five projects described in generic
    AI-generated sentences with no before/after, no scope, no value.
13. **High-friction enquiry.** A 14-checkbox service list, a budget field, a
    CAPTCHA, and a "Yes, I am located in GA / No" radio — on a form whose owner
    responds to messages the next working day.
14. **SEO doorway pages** for the same service across many towns, including
    towns they may never have served.

## 5. The category (18 competitor Atlanta landscaping sites, fetched and read)

Every one of them: a header with the phone number as a tap-to-call link, a
full-width photo of a manicured lawn, service cards numbered 01/02/03, a
"Why choose us" block, a 4-step process strip, a testimonial carousel, a
contact form with "How did you hear about us?", and a footer listing 10–22
towns. Typefaces are off-the-shelf Google fonts (Barlow, Open Sans, PT Sans,
Arvo, Roboto, DM Sans, Libre Baskerville). Palettes are forest green plus
charcoal plus white, without exception.

**Nobody in the sample:**
- puts a person in the hero (one uses stock portraits for testimonials)
- publishes what a visit costs or what it includes
- publishes a dated local seasonal calendar
- explains why a fescue lawn thins in July
- commits to a phone response time
- publishes real scope and real numbers
- mentions Atlanta landmarks, soil chemistry or climate specifics

**Responses from the operator's own records that the category ignores:**
- The phone number is the highest-value asset on the page and the least designed.
- What customers praise is the same everywhere: *the owner stays involved*,
  *he was there when he said he would be and left when he said he would*,
  *taking pictures and placing prices on tasks*, *a note after the service call*.
- What they complain about is always the same: silence, price opacity, slow
  quotes, and nobody answering.

**The one gap that is both real and documented.** A business whose only channel
is the phone loses calls while it is working. HBR's 2011 audit of 2,241
companies found an average response time of **42 hours** with **23% never
responding at all**; the 2007 Lead Response Management Study (Oldroyd with
InsideSales, 15,000+ leads) found contact within **5 minutes vs 30 minutes**
gives roughly **100× better odds of reaching** a lead and **21× better odds of
qualifying** it. Vonage (2019, 2,010 respondents) found **85% of consumers
abandon a call after reaching an auto attendant** and **51% then abandon the
business** — so an answering service has to be conversational, not an IVR.
These are the numbers behind the AI-answering half of the offer, and they are
attributed correctly (the popular "Harvard found 100×/21×" framing is wrong —
those ratios are from the 2007 LRMS study).
